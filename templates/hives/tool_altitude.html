Is the climb paced well

Add your overnight stops in order. We draw the sleeping-altitude profile and flag any night that sleeps more than 500 m higher than the night before, once you are above 3,000 m.

Common mountain-medicine advice: above 3,000 m, raise your sleeping altitude by no more than 300 to 500 m a day, and take a rest night every three to four days. Day trips higher are fine if you come down to sleep. This tool is a planning aid, not medical advice.

    How to read the flags

    Red nights, amber nights

    • Red: you sleep above 3,000 m and more than 500 m higher than the night before. Add a night in between, or a rest night lower down first.
    • Amber: a third night in a row of climbing above 3,000 m with no rest night. Consider a night at the same height.
    • Flying straight to a high airport, such as Leh at about 3,500 m, will always show red on the first night. Plan two full days of rest before going higher.
